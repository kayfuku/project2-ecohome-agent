Home battery storage lets you save electricity when it is cheap or abundant and use it when it is expensive. When paired with solar panels and time-of-use pricing, a well-managed battery can significantly reduce electricity bills and provide backup power during outages.

Charging Strategies:
- Charge the battery from surplus solar generation during midday hours
- If you have no solar, charge from the grid during off-peak hours (typically 11 PM to 6 AM)
- Avoid charging from the grid during peak pricing periods
- Use weather forecasts to decide whether to top up from the grid before a cloudy day

Discharging Strategies:
- Discharge during evening peak pricing hours (often 4 PM to 9 PM) to avoid expensive grid power
- Prioritize powering the home over exporting to the grid when export credits are low
- Enable time-of-use or "cost savings" mode in your battery app

Battery Health:
- Keep a reserve of 10-20% charge to protect battery life and provide backup power
- Avoid frequent full discharges when possible
- Install the battery in a location with moderate temperatures, as extreme heat or cold reduces performance
- Keep battery firmware up to date for improved control algorithms

Backup Power Planning:
- Increase the backup reserve before storms or forecasted grid outages
- Identify essential loads such as refrigerators, lighting and internet to run during outages
- Avoid running high-power appliances like dryers and ovens from battery during an outage

Combining with Other Devices:
- Coordinate battery discharge with EV charging so both do not draw from the grid at peak times
- Consider an EV with bidirectional charging as additional home storage
- Participate in utility virtual power plant programs to earn credits for sharing stored energy
