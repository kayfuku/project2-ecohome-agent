Energy needs change significantly throughout the year. Adjusting your habits, device settings and maintenance schedule to each season helps keep bills low and prevents unexpected spikes in heating or cooling costs.

Spring:
- Schedule an air conditioning tune-up before the cooling season begins
- Open windows on mild days to use natural ventilation instead of HVAC
- Clean solar panels after winter, as spring sunlight increases generation
- Check weatherstripping and caulking for damage from winter weather

Summer:
- Set the thermostat to 78°F when home and higher when away
- Close blinds and curtains on south- and west-facing windows during the afternoon
- Avoid using the oven during hot afternoons; grill outside or use a microwave
- Run heavy appliances in the morning or during peak solar hours, not during evening peaks

Fall:
- Schedule a heating system inspection and replace air filters
- Reverse ceiling fans to clockwise to circulate warm air
- Seal gaps around windows, doors and outlets before temperatures drop
- Lower water heater temperature to 120°F if it was raised

Winter:
- Set the thermostat to 68°F when home and 60-62°F while sleeping
- Open curtains on sunny days to capture free solar heat, and close them at night
- Use a programmable thermostat to avoid heating an empty house
- Expect lower solar generation and plan battery and EV charging accordingly

Year-Round:
- Review your monthly energy usage and compare it with the same month last year
- Track how outdoor temperature affects your consumption to plan for extreme weather
- Adjust device schedules whenever your utility changes seasonal rate periods
